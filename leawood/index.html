Keeping Your Leawood Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year if their water is hard.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or a damp spot around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but once it's gone, the water starts eating the tank steel, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a surprise cleanup.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

A water heater in Leawood almost never fails without warning. Watch for rumbling and popping while the tank heats, brownish hot water, a faint rotten-egg smell, moisture around the base, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. Those point to heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and caught early, most of them mean a modest repair or a replacement on your schedule. Left alone, they end in a failed tank and water on the floor. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it needs a professional look soon. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
