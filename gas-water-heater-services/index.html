A gas water heater is a fairly simple machine, which is part of why it lasts. A burner under the tank heats the water, exhaust rises through a flue that runs up the center of the tank, and a gas control valve on the front acts as both thermostat and safety shutoff. That valve only lets gas reach the burner while it senses a flame, through a thermocouple on standing-pilot models or a flame sensor on electronic ignition models. Most failures trace back to one of those parts. In Overland Park, KS, we regularly find thermocouples that no longer hold the valve open, pilots starved by a lint-clogged flame arrestor screen, gas valves that have failed internally, and flue or vent problems that let exhaust spill back into the room.

Every gas water heater call starts with safety and then moves to testing. If there is a gas odor, leave the home first, call the gas utility from outside, and do not operate switches or try to relight anything. Once the area is safe, our Overland Park technicians work through the heater systematically: millivolt readings on the thermocouple, a close look at the pilot and main burner flames, gas pressure at the control valve, a draft test to make sure exhaust is not spilling back, and a CO check around the appliance. Scorch marks at the burner access, rust flakes on the burner, and a lazy yellow flame each point to a different problem, and we chase down the right one.

The fix is usually straightforward once the cause is known. A new thermocouple or flame sensor, a cleaned pilot tube and burner, a cleared flame arrestor screen, or a replacement gas control valve gets most heaters running again the same day. Vent problems, like a disconnected connector or a flue pipe without enough rise, are corrected on the spot. If sediment is rumbling in the tank, we flush it, and if the anode rod is spent, we can replace it. When replacement is the smarter move, we install new gas heaters sized to your household's first-hour demand, with proper atmospheric, power vent, or direct vent venting, a drip leg on the gas supply, and the expansion tank, drain pan, and strapping your local code calls for. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Popping, rumbling, or crackling sounds as the burner heats
  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner access door
  • A rotten-egg odor near the water heater, in which case get out and call the gas company first
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • Signs of backdrafting, such as soot, condensation, or an exhaust smell at the vent
  • Water pooling around the base of the heater
  • No hot water at all, with the burner never firing
  • A pilot that lights, then goes out when you release the knob

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Gas water heaters are among the riskiest appliances to work on without training, largely because you cannot see the biggest hazards. A gas connection that is not quite tight can leak slowly for days. A vent connector that has lost its slope, or a flue partly blocked by debris, can let carbon monoxide spill into the house every time the burner fires, with no smell to warn you. Gas valve and heater replacements also typically require a permit and an inspection. Relighting a pilot by following the label is something most homeowners can do; almost everything past that deserves a technician.

Professional service gets you a correct answer and a safe appliance. We confirm thermocouple output, gas pressure, burner flame, and draft with real instruments, not trial and error. Every gas connection we make is tested for leaks before we leave, and vent connectors are secured with the right slope and clearances. When a replacement is needed, we size the new heater by first-hour rating, handle the permit where one is required, and add the expansion tank, sediment trap, drain pan, or seismic straps your home needs. The result is hot water you do not have to worry about.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here's where to begin:

  • Keep working CO alarms near bedrooms and on each floor
  • Drain sediment from the bottom of the tank once or twice a year
  • Keep the thermostat around 120 degrees to save energy and reduce scalding risk
  • Replace a spent anode rod before the tank itself starts to rust
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
  • Keep the air intake at the bottom of the heater free of lint and dust
