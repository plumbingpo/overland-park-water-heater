A tankless water heater does not store hot water at all. It waits for a faucet to open, senses the flow, fires a burner or energizes its elements, and heats water as it races through a compact heat exchanger. That on-demand design is a big part of its appeal, but it packs a lot of sensors and controls into one small cabinet, and every one of them has to agree before you get hot water. When one of them objects, the unit shuts down and posts an error code. In Overland Park, KS, the usual culprits are mineral scale coating the heat exchanger, a flow sensor that no longer registers demand, an igniter or flame rod that cannot prove the flame, and venting or gas supply problems that trip the unit's safety limits.

Overland Park Water Heater starts every tankless repair by reading the unit, not guessing at it. On every service call in Overland Park, we pull the active error code and the stored fault history, then test the components that code points to: flow sensor output while a faucet runs, flame rod signal in microamps, igniter resistance, inlet gas pressure both static and at full fire, and vent condition from the unit to the termination. Scale gets checked by comparing inlet and outlet temperatures against flow rate, since a heat exchanger that cannot transfer heat gives itself away in those numbers. A code tells us where to look, not what to replace. An ignition code, for example, can come from a worn igniter, a dirty flame rod, low gas pressure, or a blocked intake, and each one calls for a different fix.

The good news is that most tankless problems are very fixable. Scale can be flushed out, sensors and igniters are replaceable parts, and venting faults usually come down to a blocked intake or a loose joint. We carry common parts for the major brands and service both gas and electric units, so a typical tankless repair is done in one trip. We are also honest about the harder cases: a unit fed by a gas line too small for its full BTU rating will keep throwing codes until the line is upsized, and a heat exchanger damaged by scale may not be worth repairing on an older heater. And if your complaint is the cold-water sandwich, that brief burst of cold between two hot draws, we will tell you whether it is normal for your model or something we can reduce. Warning Signs to Watch For

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Small draws, like a bathroom sink, that never trigger the heater
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • The unit clicking or trying to light several times before giving up
  • Leaks under the unit or around its water connections
  • The display showing an error code instead of a temperature
  • Water that swings between hot and lukewarm in the middle of a shower
  • Hot water that cannot keep up with more than one fixture
  • A cold-water sandwich that has gotten noticeably worse

The Benefits of Professional Tankless Water Heater Repair

Start with what is at stake. A gas-fired tankless heater can burn nearly 200,000 BTU per hour at full fire, and its vent is the only thing carrying carbon monoxide out of the house; a joint that pulls loose or an intake that gets blocked changes that in a hurry. Electric tankless models are serious electrical loads, frequently more than 100 amps spread over several 240-volt circuits. Then there is the diagnostic puzzle. Error codes point to a system, not a part, so a flow sensor fault, a scaled heat exchanger, and a bad thermistor can look nearly identical on the display, and guessing gets expensive.

What you get from a professional is certainty. Instead of swapping parts, our technicians confirm the fault with a meter, a manometer, and temperature readings, so the fix is aimed at the actual cause. Scale is removed with a pump through the service valves rather than vinegar poured in and left to chance. Gas pressure is checked with the burner at full output, vent runs are inspected against the manufacturer's length and clearance limits, and the unit is tested at low and high flow before we leave. Proper service also helps you stay within the manufacturer's warranty terms, many of which expect qualified service and regular descaling.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Consider a water softener or scale-reduction filter ahead of the heater if scale keeps returning
  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
  • Leave the power on in cold weather, since freeze protection only runs when the unit has electricity
  • Write down any error code before resetting the unit, and call if it keeps coming back
  • Flush scale out of the heat exchanger annually, and sooner in hard water
  • Clear debris, snow, and dryer lint away from the vent and intake openings
  • Get the supply line checked before adding gas appliances, which can starve a tankless unit
